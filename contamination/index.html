Does an LLM's prior exposure to a collection change its relevance-review score?

Technical write-up of the contamination study in jev-eval: six collections at graded exposure, four systems, three tests (case knowledge, document memorisation, knowledge effect under rename-and-subtract), four generalisation checks, a renamer audit, and the decisions taken along the way.

Prepared 2026-10-07 for the applied-science reader. Every number is taken from design/06_contamination_probe.md, design/07_ablation_round2.md, results/contam/, results/ablation/, results/verify/ and results/jev_probe/; nothing here is estimated from memory. Figures are the project's own PNGs; relative paths resolve from results/contam/. Total project spend $300.09.

One-paragraph result. Across Enron, Jeb Bush and CUAD, under three manipulations (rename every name, paraphrase the text, inject a case brief), knowing the case changed review F1 by at most a few points for the three GPT-5.6 models: knowledge effects within ±2.6 F1, one of nine LLM intervals off zero (Terra on Jeb Bush, −2.3 [−4.3, −0.3]). The models know the cases thoroughly (80–90 % of a fact checklist on Enron, 100 % identification of nameless sketches) but show no document-level memorisation of any e-mail collection (finish-the-passage overlap at the synthetic floor; longest exact runs 2–4 words; CUAD contracts are the exception at 10 % of windows with a ≥ 15-word run for Sol). Renaming is a weak blindfold: the renamed Enron and Jeb Bush e-mails were still attributed to the right company or office 60–94 % of the time, so every knowledge effect is a lower bound. Jev, the decision model, carries real-world knowledge of the matters at effect sizes comparable to the LLMs (code-name swap) and a small, real familiarity effect on Jeb Bush (−3.1 [−5.5, −0.8]), with no sign of memorised documents or labels. One renamer defect (Veridian v1) produced a spurious 2–3-point "renaming cost" and three spurious significant contrasts; it was found by audit, fixed and re-run, and the headline did not move.

1. Purpose, research question, exposure typology

The main eval compares Jev (a commercial relevance-review decision model) with generative LLMs on document review across several corpora. Four of those corpora are public and old enough to have been in a frontier model's pre-training data; the two e-mail collections most used in eDiscovery research (Enron, Jeb Bush) were posted on the open web long before any of these models were trained. The question this study answers is narrow and operational:

Research question. If a system has been exposed to a collection, to the cast of people in it, to the benchmark that reuses it, to its published relevance judgments, or to the matter behind it (the litigation, its facts and players), does that exposure change the system's relevance-review score on that collection in a way that would not transfer to a client's never-seen matter? Measure the exposure per channel; then measure its consequence for F1.

The design separates exposure (what the model knows; Tests 1 and 2) from effect (what that knowledge does to the score; Test 3). The first is cheap to measure with short prompts and no document classification; the second needs paired classification runs. Both are read against a floor (a collection no model can know) and, for exposure, a ceiling (text every model has certainly memorised).

Two kinds of knowing fall out of this typology and the study keeps them apart throughout. Knowing the documents (verbatim memorisation, knowing the cast, knowing the benchmark's topics, knowing the published labels) is what the word "contamination" usually means. Knowing the case (recognising the matter from a nameless sketch, recalling its record, knowing what responsive documents will contain before opening one) is the channel specific to eDiscovery, since four of the six collections come from famous litigation. Why three tests and not one

Each test answers a question the others cannot. A verbatim probe (Test 2) that finds nothing clears a corpus only at the document level; a model can know the Enron fraud cold without being able to finish a single Enron e-mail, and that knowledge could still tell it what responsive documents look like. The case-knowledge probes (Test 1) measure that channel but say nothing about whether it does anything. Test 3 measures the consequence directly, by removing the names (or paraphrasing the text, or injecting a brief) and measuring the paired change in F1. The three are read together: exposure is common at the matter level and rare at the document level for internal e-mail, and we could not detect a consequence of the former for review accuracy beyond a few F1 points.

Statistics

Every ablation delta is paired by document: the same document is classified in both conditions, and the bootstrap resamples documents (clusters), not decisions — 2,000 draws for the ablation and checks, 1,000 for the PR small-multiple figures, 500 for the per-request tables. CUAD clusters by contract (102 clusters), because renaming is a per-contract treatment. Knowledge effects are differences of bootstrap delta distributions drawn jointly (matter and control requests on the same resampled documents). Jev: the code-name swap (T1) and why v1 was confounded

The generative probes cannot be posed to a classifier, so Jev's case knowledge is measured through its own interface (ediscovery_bench/jevprobe/). T1 uses 40 templated documents per matter (Enron, Jeb Bush, Mallinckrodt, Endo), each in two versions that differ in exactly one token: a real matter token (Raptor, Schiavo, Exalgo, Opana ER) versus a fictional one of the same shape (Tercel, Petrossi, Veltrano, Veltrex ER). The request describes the conduct and never names the token. Signal pairs: a case-aware reader calls the real version relevant more often. Decoy pairs (Azurix, FCAT, Ofirmev, Lidoderm): the real token is something a case-aware reader knows is not what was asked, so knowledge should lower the call. The decoy design is what separates knowledge from a generic "real-looking name" effect, and the signal − decoy contrast is the number to quote.

The v1 confound. v1 sent each matter's task-YAML context as written. A word-boundary audit of every T1 token against the context and all request fields found the Mallinckrodt context named Exalgo, Roxicodone, Methadose and the decoys Ofirmev, INOmax, Acthar ("branded opioids including…", "non-opioid products (e.g.…) that are not at issue"), and the Endo context named Opana ER, Qualitest and the decoys Lidoderm, Voltaren Gel, Aveed, Supprelin. On those two matters a real token could match the prompt rather than training knowledge, in both directions. Check C had shown the same thing independently (with the original Endo context Jev followed counterfactual text 57 % of the time; 90 % with a nameless one). v2 replaced the two contexts with the product-nameless versions written for Check C, re-ran Mallinckrodt and Endo on all four systems ($0.51), carried Enron and Jeb Bush over unchanged (their prompts were already token-free), and Matter.taskset() now raises if any token appears in a context or request. v1 is archived at results/jev_probe/t1_v1_confounded/.

Reading. With token-free prompts the classifier's call still depends on recognising real-world tokens at effect sizes within the LLMs' range on three of four matters. This is inconsistent with a reading of the vendor statement under which Jev has no real-world knowledge, and consistent with either pre-training on public text or synthetic training data generated by a model that has that knowledge; the test cannot separate those two, and it does not show benchmark contamination: knowing what Exalgo is, is not having seen the Mallinckrodt collection. The bare-token check (FAS 140: a header plus one sentence with Raptor/LJM2/Chewco vs invented twins) gives 0 % / 0 % with the request as written and 78 % / 0 % once the criteria name the vehicles, so the ablation's −18 F1 on FAS 140 under renaming is not a bare-name reflex but Jev reading request and document together.

  • T3 paraphrase sensitivity (Jev only; ~100 documents each from Enron, Endo, Veridian, original vs Luna paraphrase).   • T4 published vs unpublished labels. Overall: Jev uses real-world matter knowledge (T1) and shows no sign of remembering the benchmark's documents or labels (T2–T4). Both halves are evidence, not proof.

Verbatim continuation: design, pool quality, scoring

The probe is the reproduction test from NYT v. OpenAI applied to review collections: show the model the start of a document and ask it to finish it exactly. Two scores per window: LCS-F1, the F1 of the longest common word subsequence between the model's 60 words and the true continuation (0 = nothing shared, 1 = every word in order), and the longest exact run of consecutive matching words, with the novel variant counting only runs that contain a 5-gram absent from the prompt (digits masked). The run statistic is the diagnostic one: genre-predictable text (contract boilerplate, machine-written marketing prose) inflates LCS-F1 without producing long party-specific runs, and the ≥ 15-word run threshold is where boilerplate stops being able to supply the right specific tokens.

Pool quality. The first pool was 60 random windows per corpus, and inspection of its high scorers showed three kinds of chaff: boilerplate (disclaimers, signatures, forwarded-header blocks, out-of-office replies), public text reproduced inside the corpus (Federal Register notices, statutes, press releases, newsletters), and low-information windows (header-only openings; continuations predictable from the prefix such as tables, attendee lists and repeated error logs). The e-mail corpora's longest run — Sol reproducing 34 consecutive words of a Mallinckrodt e-mail — turned out to be a DEA Paperwork Reduction Act notice quoted in the e-mail: memorisation of the Federal Register, not of the collection. Two filters are now applied by builder and scorer alike: a rules layer (boilerplate.py: disclaimer, signature, quoted header, unsubscribe, auto-reply, scanner notice, protective-order legend, degenerate repetition, key:value lists, tabular, OCR garbage, thin prefix, and two corpus-frequency guards from an 8-gram document-frequency index) and an LLM screen (Luna at temperature 0 assigns original_internal / public_reproduction / template_boilerplate / low_information; only the first enters the pool; verdicts cached in v_screen.jsonl). The ceiling texts are exempt from public_reproduction by design. Replacement windows are drawn deterministically until the pool is 60; the resumable runner executes only the new ids. Effect: e-mail-corpus LCS-F1 moved by ≤ 0.05, mean longest runs fell from 3–4 words to 2.1–2.6, and no e-mail window in the final pool has a ≥ 15-word run for any model (longest 10, a sales report whose parallel structure the prompt gives away).

Refusals. A review-page inspection found Luna continuations beginning "I'm sorry, but I can't provide the continuation of a private email…" untagged, because the old detector tested for a straight apostrophe and GPT-5.6 uses a curly one (4 of 37 caught). The new detector requires a first-person refusal opening and a named withheld object within 30 words, categorised private / copyright / other; candidate phrasings were clustered from the data. Only Luna refuses, and sharply by corpus: Veridian 25 %, Endo 15 %, Mallinckrodt 3 %, Enron 0 %, founding documents 0 %. Modern-looking product-liability e-mail reads as "private" regardless of whether it is real. Treatment: every V statistic is computed over answered items; the refusals-as-zero mean is kept as lcs_f_mean_all. Under the old convention the undetected refusals, concentrated on the floor corpus, had deflated Luna's floor to 0.135 and made Enron look significantly above it (p = 0.001); on answered items the floor is 0.152 and no e-mail corpus is above it for Luna (p = 0.16–0.53).

What non-recitation does and does not prove

Failing to recite does not prove a model never saw a document. Memorisation tracks repetition, not presence: the Enron corpus is in The Pile, so presence in training is likely, and single-exposure e-mails are evidently not reproduced even by the largest model, while the Federalist Papers (replicated millions of times) are. The verbatim result is therefore "no evidence of document-level memorisation under a probe that does detect memorisation elsewhere" (the canon, Titanic, CUAD), not "the model has not read the e-mails". The calibration note of 2026-10-04 changed every write-up to that wording. The consequence for review accuracy is measured directly in Test 3, which is why the study does not rest on this test. CUAD remains the one collection where memorised text is present, and its paraphrase arm (§5) is the test of whether that text does anything.

Endo as the real-world post-cutoff check. A production published after every model's cutoff, from a matter they can know: V LCS-F1 0.15 / 0.14 / 0.15 against Veridian's 0.13–0.17; entity recall 0/30; recognition d′ 0.6 / −1.1 / −0.7; label recall 47–52 %; but M0 identified by all three and M1 70 / 55 / 70 %. The clearest "known case, unseen documents" profile in the study, Enron's pattern from the other side, and the recommended held-out benchmark going forward (caveat: its gold is a three-OpenAI-model panel).

5. Test 3 — does knowing the case change the score?

Definition and sign convention

Classify a fixed sample twice — once as-is (named), once with every person, organisation and place consistently replaced by invented ones (renamed) — and compare F1 per request, paired by document. Renaming has a mechanical cost of its own (the model loses nothing it knows but has to read unfamiliar tokens), so the raw Δ is never the quantity of interest. Two subtractions remove it:

Knowledge effect = ΔF1(renamed − named) on matter requests − ΔF1(renamed − named) on control requests, on the same documents, drawn jointly in each bootstrap resample.

Sign. Negative = the real names were worth something on the matter requests (renaming hurt them more than it hurt the controls). Zero = they were not. Positive = renaming hurt the controls more, which is noise or a renamer artefact, not knowledge.

Cross-corpus variant (when no in-corpus control exists): ΔF1(corpus) − ΔF1(Veridian renamed), the pure renaming floor. Loose, because the two renamers are different treatments; used for CUAD and as a secondary column elsewhere.

Matter vs control requests: Enron Complaint J (seven TREC 2010 requests: prepay transactions, FAS 140, financial analysts, document destruction, energy schedules, financial forecasts, plus fantasy football as an in-mailbox control) versus Complaint K on the same mailbox (an unrelated oil-spill fact pattern, three requests); Jeb Bush matter topics (recount 2000, Rilya Wilson, Medicaid reform, George W. The renamer

ediscovery_bench/ablation/names.py (Renamer) is a deterministic dictionary with four classes: phrases (organisations, products, places, domains; longest-first, case-insensitive), rare surnames (replaced wherever capitalised), common-word surnames (words that are also English words — Lee, Park, Grant, Reid — replaced only in full-name position after a known first name or on header lines), and first names (kept by design: "Ken" alone carries no identification). E-mail display names, Lotus paths and Exchange aliases are handled; local parts follow the surname. Enron's people table was harvested from header fields (≈ 2,300 surnames); Jeb Bush's maps the Governor and family, the 2000 tickets and litigation, and 715 public figures while keeping Florida, Tallahassee, agencies and statutes (renaming the state would break the topical relevance of most requests); CUAD's ContractRenamer maps parties and their defined-term aliases, products, signature-block people, US-state jurisdictions, every date (year shifted −3..+3 per contract) and every dollar amount (× 0.6–1.5, re-rounded). v2 fixes (2026-10-06, after the audit in §7): bare short forms of mapped products (Apex → Summit), request-named surnames forced into the replace-everywhere class, role words (Counsel, Manager, General, Quality) excluded from the name tables, title and initial positions ("Dr. Feld", "D. Mitchell"), three-letter surnames in local parts (mlee@ → mhan@), underscore as a word boundary. The Big Thorium mapping was built with v2 and the cast's dictionary-word surnames (Clark, Smith, Baker, Carter, Reed, Archer) forced rare from the start; its residual audit was zero before any renamed run.

Brief injection: adding knowledge instead of removing it

The converse manipulation. Append a one-page case brief to the context and classify the same documents again. Three arms: Mallinckrodt (a real brief, 1,840 documents × 8 requests, round 1), Veridian (Check D: a 1,431-token fictional brief — parties, products, people, deals and code names, timeline, outcome — on a stratified 491-document half), Big Thorium (Relativity's own aiR case summary, 1,059 words, on a 300-document positive-enriched subset).

Circularity caveat on the panel-gold arms (Mallinckrodt, Big Thorium): the gold is the panel's without-brief majority, so a system that follows the brief away from the panel is scored as wrong; the brief effect there is a lower bound on agreement change, not an accuracy change. Veridian's gold is independent of the systems. The pattern is the same in all three: handing a system the one kind of knowledge it cannot have does not move F1 beyond noise; what it buys the decision model is a few points of recall at the cost of precision.

Carry-forward wording. For the LLMs, knowing the case changed review accuracy by at most a few F1 points holds on three matters (Enron, Jeb Bush, CUAD) under three manipulations (remove names, paraphrase, inject a brief); no knowledge effect beyond 2.6 F1 points; every interval includes zero except Terra on Jeb Bush. For Jev, a small familiarity effect of ≈ 2–3 points on matter topics built around public figures (Jeb Bush −3.1; FAS 140; the knowledge-dependent Enron documents in Check A), consistent with public-web knowledge of public figures and not by itself evidence of training on the collections. Every effect is a lower bound because renaming is a weak blindfold.

  • Round-2 "rename effect: CUAD − Veridian": Terra +2.2 † → −0.3, Sol +2.5 † → +1.2 [−0.1, +2.5], Jev +1.9 † → +0.3; all through zero. The "CUAD renaming artefact" the study had been explaining disappeared with the floor.
  • Δ(Jeb all) − Δ(Veridian): all through zero.
  • The sentence reading Terra's Jeb Bush −2.3 as "of the order of the Veridian floor" is withdrawn; the floor is −0.4.
  • Unchanged: the Enron and Jeb Bush knowledge effects (same-collection controls, no Veridian dependence); the headline (within ±2.6, one of nine LLM intervals off zero); every verbatim, entity, label and matter-knowledge result.
  • Sol keeps a small genuine renaming cost on Veridian (−1.0, interval just off zero) — the allowance to make when reading Sol's renaming results.
Two methodological lessons. (1) A leaky perturbation mimics lost knowledge exactly. A document with the product renamed in one sentence and not the next looks, to the classifier, like a document about something it half-recognises, and the score drops on precisely the requests that mention the unmapped token. The signature is per-request concentration of recall loss on documents carrying residuals; the remedy is a residual audit to zero before any renamed run (now standard: Big Thorium's mapping was audited to zero first). (2) Renaming is a weak blindfold. Even with a clean renamer, the models attributed renamed Enron e-mails to Enron 60–94 % of the time and renamed Jeb Bush e-mails to his office 69–94 % of the time, from domains, places, dates and vocabulary. Every rename-and-subtract knowledge effect is therefore a lower bound; the brief-injection arms and Check C are the complementary designs that do not depend on blinding.

8. Decision log

Chronological. Each entry gives the decision and the reason it was taken, as recorded in the design docs and the working transcript.

Use Veridian as the floor for every probe; add explicit positive controls after the first pass (2026-10-03).
The first draft used Enron as the verbatim ceiling on the grounds that it is in The Pile. The run showed single-exposure e-mails are not reproduced even by the largest model, so a corpus whose status was the thing under test could not serve as a ceiling. The founding documents (Project Gutenberg) and the Kaggle Titanic CSV were added: text replicated millions of times, so saturation is guaranteed and the scale gradient (Luna 0.36 → Terra 0.63 → Sol 0.94) calibrates every other reading.
Treat the vendor's "trained on synthetic data" statement as a claim under test, never a premise.
The study compares Jev with the LLMs; assuming the comparison system is clean would beg the question. Jev is run through every arm its interface allows (ablation, M3, T1–T4, checks A/C/D), and every result is reported as consistent or inconsistent with the statement. The T1 result (real-world token knowledge at LLM-scale effect sizes) is compatible with the statement only if the synthetic data was generated by a model that has that knowledge, which is the natural way to make such data; the test cannot separate the two.
Rebuild the verbatim pool with a rules layer plus an LLM screen (2026-10-04).
Inspection of the top-scoring windows showed boilerplate, embedded public text (the 34-word Federal Register run) and prompt-copyable continuations. A good V item is custodian-authored, matter-specific text with an unpredictable continuation. Verdicts are cached so re-runs are free; replacement windows cost $0.57. Conclusions held on the better pool; the ceiling texts are exempt from the public-reproduction class by design.
Score verbatim statistics over answered items only; report refusals separately (2026-10-04).
Only Luna refuses, and it refuses most on the floor corpus (25 % on Veridian), so scoring refusals as zero deflated the floor and made Enron look significantly above it. A refusal is a policy reading, not a memorisation reading.
Balance label-recall pairs within topic, not across the corpus.
Sol scored 70 % on Mallinckrodt by answering "relevant" to every *_broad topic; the question text carried label information. Pairs are now balanced per topic and the report prints the topic-only ceiling (50 %) beside the accuracy.
Define the knowledge effect as a within-corpus difference (matter − control on the same documents), with the Veridian floor as a secondary cross-corpus column.
Renaming has a mechanical cost that differs by corpus and renamer. The same-documents subtraction removes it exactly; the Veridian subtraction removes it only approximately (different treatment), which is why the Veridian renamer defect could distort the cross-corpus columns without touching the Enron or Jeb Bush knowledge effects.
Strip EDRM production headers (X-SDOC, X-ZLID) from both Enron conditions.
The leak check showed models naming Enron from them. Stripping in both conditions keeps the pair comparable.
Add a second perturbation (paraphrase) for CUAD; measure the removed surface directly.
CUAD is the one corpus with document-level memorisation and benchmark knowledge; renaming parties cannot remove memorised text. The finish-the-document probe on the paraphrased windows (Sol 0.315 → 0.279; ≥ 15-word runs 10 % → 6 %) proves the paraphrase removed what it was meant to remove, so the null effect on F1 is informative.
Rename Jeb Bush's people, not Florida.
Renaming the state would break the topical relevance of most requests (Tallahassee, DCF, AHCA, Florida statutes). The mapping removes identification of the Governor, his family, the 2000 litigation and 715 public figures; the leak check then measures what is left (69–94 % still identified).
Run round 2 as the cheap version first (Terra dropped under the $75 rule), add Terra when the key was topped up (2026-10-04 → 10-06).
Budget rule: full > $75 → run the version without the mid model first; Luna and Sol span the price/quality range and Sol carries the strongest memorisation signal. Terra's six runs ($26.62, each within 2 % of estimate) completed the table and produced the one off-zero LLM interval.
Redesign T1 with token-free contexts (v2) and assert it in code (2026-10-04).
A prompt audit found the Mallinckrodt and Endo task contexts named the very tokens being swapped, in both signal and decoy roles, so a real token could match the prompt. Check C had found the same independently. The v2 contexts are the product-nameless ones written for Check C; Enron and Jeb Bush predictions are carried over because their prompts were already clean; v1 is archived. The reading holds via the signal − decoy contrast (4 of 4 off zero in both versions).
Run the four generalisation checks as designs that could each undermine the claim (2026-10-04).
A claim defended only by the experiments that produced it is weak. A and D test the mechanism directly; B is the weakest design and is reported because it came out ambiguous; C shows the strong form of the claim is false and bounds how much that matters.
Calibrate every write-up against overclaiming (2026-10-04).
Standard: state what was observed, name the inference it supports, say what would overturn it. "They have not read the e-mails" → "no evidence of document-level memorisation under a probe that detects it elsewhere"; "the effect is zero" → a bound with intervals through zero; "every model, every matter" → "26 of 27, the miss named"; correlations reported per model with significance.
Audit every renamed set when the Veridian result looked wrong; fix and re-run only Veridian (2026-10-06).
Fictional-to-fictional renaming cannot cost 2–3 points. The audit tied 87 % of lost positives to residual tokens. Veridian is the floor for three published columns, so it was re-run ($14.21). Enron J/K ($9.4) and Jeb Bush ($15.4) were not: their defects are cosmetic, the knowledge effects on clean documents are the same within noise, and no interval changes status. CUAD's rename residue affects identification only; its paraphrase infidelity is a caveat, not a fix.
Add Big Thorium as a sixth collection; describe it as public documents, invented case (2026-10-06).
A second floor on which document exposure is possible closes the gap between Veridian (private, invented) and CUAD (public, no case). A web search found no indexed page naming the set and the worker first described it as internal; the user confirmed Relativity distributes it as the aiR demo workspace and the wording was corrected everywhere. The empirical point survives either way: no model recites, recognises or recalls it.
Big Thorium sampling 650 / 150 / 200 with the 18 human positives forced in; panel gold with leave-one-out.
Nine in ten messages are off-topic filler, so a random 1,000 would carry too few matter documents; stratifying by matter-keyword / control-keyword / other (seed 20261006) gives the requests enough positives while keeping controls. The demo's own coding (18 positives, one coder, a BBQ invitation marked Responsive) cannot support recall intervals, so the Mallinckrodt/Endo panel procedure is reused with each LLM scored against the other two to remove self-circularity; agreement with the demo coding is reported as a check (15/18).
Keep Jev's +4.3 Big Thorium "knowledge effect" out of the article.
Wrong sign for knowledge, entirely from five control-request positives sitting within 0.1 of the threshold (83 % of Jev's flips vs 0–5 % for the LLMs). It is a surface-token sensitivity of the decision model and is documented in the report with that explanation.
Reinterpret Terra's Jeb Bush −2.3 after the Veridian fix.
With the renaming floor at −0.4 rather than −2.9, the −2.3 can no longer be called renaming cost. It is small but real, and the pages and article say so; the conditional wording in effect2.py and story.py is generated from the data.
What the article claims and does not claim.
Claims: the models know the cases; no document-level memorisation of any e-mail collection; knowing the case changed review accuracy by at most a few F1 points, one of nine LLM intervals off zero; renaming is a weak blindfold so the effects are lower bounds; a brief does not help; Jev carries real-world matter knowledge and a small familiarity effect on public-figure topics, with no sign of memorised documents or labels. Does not claim: that the models never saw the e-mails; that the effect is zero; that the vendor statement is true or false; anything about Anthropic or Gemini models; anything beyond these matters at these sample sizes.
